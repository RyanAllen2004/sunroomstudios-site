Printable Christmas party games: how to run a game night that runs itself

A printable game night lives or dies on setup. Here is a simple way to plan one for Christmas that takes about an hour of prep and then mostly looks after itself.

1. Pick two or three formats, not ten

Variety keeps a mixed-age crowd interested, but more than three formats gets fiddly. A good mix is one team game, one quick-fire game and one that people can dip in and out of while they eat.

  • A team puzzle game. An escape-room-style printable gives groups something to solve together. The shop has the Christmas Escape Room (DIG-009).
  • A move-around game. A scavenger hunt gets people off the sofa. See the Scavenger Hunt listing and check its page for theme and details.
  • A sit-down game. Trivia and bingo work well around a table. See Trivia & Bingo and check its page for theme and details.

If you would rather buy one thing than three, the Christmas Games Bundle (DIG-015) is the shop’s bundle listing. Check the listing for exactly which games it contains.

2. Do the printing the day before

  • Print one test page first, at 100% scale, to check margins before you print everything.
  • Print an extra copy of anything that gets written on, because someone always makes a mistake.
  • Put each game in its own folder or envelope so you can hand it out in one go.

3. Set the table before guests arrive

Pencils beat pens for puzzles. Add a few clipboards or hardback books for people not sitting at the table, a timer someone can see, and small prizes if you like. They do not need to be big; a bar of chocolate does the job.

4. Run the evening in short rounds

Fifteen to twenty minutes per game is a comfortable pace for most groups. Announce each round’s start and end, keep score on a visible sheet, and stop a game while people are still enjoying it rather than when they are bored.

5. Make it work for mixed ages

Pair children with adults on team games, and have a quieter option available for anyone who does not want to compete. A free word search makes a good fallback; there is a free Christmas Market word search to try.
